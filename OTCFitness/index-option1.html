Option 1 · Library-led

The training content,
your data, your food
and the coach.

The athlete picks a guide from Steve's library and works through it in their own time. The app doesn't schedule them — it gives them the knowledge, reads their wearable, tracks what they eat, and answers questions about all of it.

12 interconnected screens No generated schedule Light & dark Tap anything to navigate

Every discipline on the OTCF site, delivered as PDF, video and audio modules from the admin console.

Wearable data flows in automatically. Nothing to log, nothing to tick off.

Nutrition targets move with what the watch actually recorded, not with a prescribed session.

01

What changes from Option 2

One module comes out: the plan engine. Everything else in the app is untouched. That single removal takes the dated calendar, the structured session screen and the completion tracking with it.

Option 1 — the athlete chooses. They browse the library, open a guide, and work through its modules whenever suits. Progress is measured by what they've read and watched. There is no schedule to fall behind.

In scope: training library with PDF / video / audio · Apple Health & Health Connect · nutrition module with AI meal logging · AI Coach · analytics · admin console.

Option 2 — the app prescribes. Steve authors a plan per category, the athlete activates one, and the app hands them weekly training tasks with structured sessions. They tick each one off, and compliance becomes a real metric the coach can talk about.

Adds: plan builder in admin · plan activation & personalisation · dated calendar · structured session screen · mark-as-complete · auto-match from wearable · reschedule tools · compliance analytics.

Nothing is lost from the library in Option 2 — it contains everything in Option 1 and adds the scheduled layer on top. Option 1 is the smaller first release, not a different product.

Pushing structured workouts out to a Garmin or Wahoo is Phase 2 in both options, and only becomes possible once the plan engine from Option 2 exists.

Option 1 prices lower because there is less to sell — a library subscription, not a coaching programme. Worth modelling before choosing.

02

Getting started

Four onboarding steps instead of seven. Nothing needs collecting in order to build a plan, so the athlete reaches the library — and their first bit of value — far sooner.

03

The training library

In Option 1 this is the product. Guides are measured in modules and running time rather than weeks, and every module carries its PDF, video and audio.

04

Coach & nutrition

Unchanged from Option 2, and still the differentiator. The coach reads training, sleep and fuelling together — and can point the athlete at the module that answers their question.

05

Insight & conversion

Analytics reports what the athlete actually recorded. Without a plan there is no planned-versus-actual and no compliance figure — that is the one real analytical loss in Option 1.
